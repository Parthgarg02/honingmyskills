Recently during one of my training programs we were discussing this serious question. To which everyone has their own thoughts some people thought that the social media platform owns our data and some thought that we own our data.

![Graphic reading "Who owns your data? Ideally, you should."](/images/posts/who-owns-our-social-media-data-1.webp)

This activity was mind boggling activity as I took the stand that we own our data, but the person who hosted this activity gave few insights which made me think that do we really own our data.

Now the conclusion to this whole activity was that we do own our data, but we can do nothing if that data is used by the social media in the name of research and development, or in the name of quality purpose, or whatever reason they quote (So basically we are owners in the name only).

Now they are selling our data and that’s why they are giving us these platforms for free (Data is the new oil), and we can do nothing about it if we want to use their platform.

![Diagram of content flowing from social media platforms to marketing agencies, with "You" separate from it](/images/posts/who-owns-our-social-media-data-2.webp)

But the problem does not end here, the point is they ask for your permission to access your whole photo gallery. So let’s assume you have not uploaded any data but still if they want they can access your whole gallery, and use that for “Research & Development”.

Now your gallery may contain your adhaar card photo, pan card photo, or maybe your bank details as well. Are you willing to share those details with any of your friends as well?? If not then why a complete group of strangers??

Do think about this. Now Apple listens to their users and they have come up with selected photos access to these kinds of applications. Where you can select few photos and your social media account won’t be able to access any other photo. I find this feature useful. Let’s see when we can see a similar feature on Android.

What do you guys think about this. I am open to any discussion.
