Apple Pay is finally in India. It went live on 30 September 2026, about 12 years after it launched in the US.

It's been a long wait, and the RBI's rules were a big part of it. In 2021 the RBI's card tokenisation rules came in. Apple stopped taking credit and debit cards for App Store and iCloud purchases in India around that time, and card payments only started coming back this July.

After a lot of conversations and regulatory compliance, Apple Pay is now here. But only with Axis Bank for now.

## What works right now

- Axis Bank credit cards on Visa and Mastercard
- Payments in shops, in apps and on websites, from iPhone, Apple Watch and iPad (Mac support is coming soon)
- Your card number isn't shared with the shop. Apple Pay stores an encrypted token instead.

## What doesn't work yet

- RuPay cards
- Debit cards
- UPI
- Other banks. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and SBI Card are not part of the launch.
- Every terminal. Reportedly, a payment can work at one terminal and fail at another if that terminal's acquiring bank hasn't enabled it.

## Why only Axis for now

Axis is India's fourth-largest credit card issuer, with about 16.26 million active cards as of July. That's a lot of people, but it also means many card holders still can't use Apple Pay.

HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and SBI Card are still negotiating commercial terms with Apple. Apple reportedly wants 15 to 20 basis points on each transaction, and banks have been pushing back at around 10. That could be why they aren't live yet. These numbers are from reports, and neither Apple nor the banks have confirmed them.

## What this means for UPI

Apple Pay isn't going to replace UPI, and it doesn't really try to. UPI is more than 80% of digital payment volume in India, and PhonePe and Google Pay between them handle about 80% of that. Apple Pay works differently. It runs on credit cards through the card networks, while UPI moves money straight from your bank account.

So the people it's built for are credit card users, and what it offers them is convenience and security, with no OTP at checkout. For everyday small payments, most of us will keep using UPI.

UPI is also moving into Apple Pay's territory. NPCI has launched UPI Tap & Pay, so you can now tap your phone and pay through UPI too.

There's a cost angle on the UPI side too. Since 15 October, UPI is no longer completely free for merchants. NPCI has put a 0.4% fee on eligible merchant payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 per transaction. Payments up to ₹2,000, which are more than 95% of UPI merchant transactions, stay free, and so do person-to-person transfers and small merchants.

Credit cards are also losing share to UPI in merchant payments, even as card spending keeps growing.

## My experience so far

I've been using it, and it works well. I've paid with it on Swiggy and Zepto, and the experience was seamless. I also use it to tap on POS machines.

For now, Apple Pay is for people who have an Axis Bank credit card and an Apple device. Let's see how it goes from here.
